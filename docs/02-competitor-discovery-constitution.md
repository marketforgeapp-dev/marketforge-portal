# MarketForge Competitor Constitution
### Version 2.0
### Living Document

---

# Purpose

Competitor discovery exists to establish trust.

For most customers, the competitor list is one of the first things they see after onboarding.

If the competitors are obviously wrong,

confidence in everything that follows is reduced.

If the competitors accurately reflect the businesses a customer truly competes against,

trust begins immediately.

Competitor discovery is not simply a data collection exercise.

It is the first demonstration that MarketForge understands the customer's business.

---

# Mission

The purpose of competitor discovery is to accurately identify the businesses most likely to win work instead of our customer.

Everything else is secondary.

---

# Constitutional Principles

## I. Customer Trust Comes First

Competitor discovery is the first trust interface.

Every competitor surfaced should increase customer confidence that:

"MarketForge understands my market."

One obviously incorrect competitor can damage trust far more than several correct competitors can build it.

Accuracy is more important than quantity.

---

## II. Consumer Choice Defines Competition

A competitor is not defined by geography.

A competitor is not defined by Google ranking.

A competitor is not defined by identical categories.

A competitor is:

**A business that a customer would realistically choose instead of our customer.**

Consumer choice is the foundation of competitor discovery.

---

## III. Service Area Matters More Than Office Location

Local service businesses travel.

Customers purchase service,

not office proximity.

Service areas should take precedence over physical addresses whenever possible.

Office location alone is rarely sufficient to determine competition.

---

## IV. Awareness Matters

Well-known businesses influence customer decisions.

Brand recognition.

Reputation.

Review volume.

Market presence.

Advertising.

Longevity.

These all contribute to consumer choice.

Market awareness is a legitimate competitive signal.

---

## V. Capability Before Similarity

Businesses compete because customers perceive them as capable of solving the same problem.

Identical Google categories are insufficient.

Recommendations should consider:

- services offered
- specialization
- business positioning
- customer perception
- real-world substitution

Competition exists when customers would reasonably compare two businesses.

---

## VI. One Brand Is One Competitor

Customers think in brands.

Not office locations.

Not duplicate listings.

Not franchise records.

Each competitor should represent one recognizable business.

Duplicate brands reduce confidence and add no value.

---

## VII. Quality Over Quantity

A small set of highly relevant competitors is more valuable than a large list of weak matches.

Every surfaced competitor should earn its place.

Adding competitors simply to reach an arbitrary number weakens the overall experience.

---

## VIII. Competitor Discovery Must Be Explainable

Customers should understand why a competitor appears.

If asked:

"Why is this company considered my competitor?"

The answer should be understandable.

Competitor selection should never appear random.

---

## IX. Multiple Signals Create Better Intelligence

No single metric defines competition.

Competitor discovery should combine signals such as:

- services
- business categories
- service area
- review volume
- ratings
- market awareness
- proximity when appropriate
- website context
- business positioning
- structured business information

Robust conclusions require multiple forms of evidence.

---

## X. AI Supports Discovery

Artificial intelligence may:

- enrich understanding
- interpret websites
- infer positioning
- improve relevance

AI should never replace deterministic safeguards.

Structured evidence remains the foundation.

---

## XI. Deterministic Validation Protects Trust

AI may suggest.

Rules validate.

Duplicate brands.

Irrelevant industries.

Businesses outside realistic competition.

Poor-quality matches.

These should be filtered before customers see them whenever practical.

---

## XII. Competitor Discovery Is About Business Reality

The purpose is not to mirror Google.

The purpose is not to reproduce search rankings.

The purpose is not to maximize API results.

The purpose is to accurately model the businesses customers actually compete against.

---

## XIII. Industry Neutrality Is Required

Competitor discovery must function consistently across industries.

Never hard-code assumptions for:

- plumbing
- HVAC
- septic
- tree service
- electrical
- roofing
- landscaping

Industry understanding should emerge from business context,

not product bias.

---

## XIV. Competitors Are Context, Not Scores

Competitors exist to improve MarketForge intelligence.

They provide context for:

- opportunity discovery
- market positioning
- pricing awareness
- messaging
- competitive differentiation
- growth recommendations

Competitor discovery is an input into better decisions.

It is not an end product.

---

## XV. Freshness Matters

Markets evolve.

Businesses open.

Businesses close.

Services change.

Reviews grow.

Competitor discovery should refresh frequently enough to remain representative without creating unnecessary instability.

Recommendations should balance freshness with consistency.

---

## XVI. Stability Builds Confidence

Minor market fluctuations should not radically change the competitor list.

Owners should recognize their competitors over time.

Large changes should occur only when meaningful changes in the market justify them.

Consistency reinforces trust.

---

## XVII. Every Competitor Must Add Value

Every surfaced competitor should improve MarketForge's understanding of the customer's market.

If removing a competitor would not materially reduce market understanding,

that competitor probably does not belong.

---

## XVIII. Competitor Intelligence Exists to Improve Growth

Competitor discovery is not about surveillance.

It is not about copying competitors.

It exists to better understand:

- customer choice
- market dynamics
- competitive strengths
- competitive weaknesses
- opportunity gaps

The goal is always to improve customer growth.

---

## XIX. Simplicity Creates Confidence

Customers should immediately recognize most competitors.

The experience should feel obvious.

Owners should think:

"Yes... those are exactly the businesses I compete with."

Not:

"Why are they here?"

Recognition builds trust.

Confusion erodes it.

---

## XX. Competitor Discovery Must Earn Trust Every Time

Every onboarding.

Every refresh.

Every market update.

Every new workspace.

Competitor discovery should reinforce one idea:

**MarketForge understands this business.**

Without that confidence,

everything downstream becomes harder.

---

# Competitor Decision Framework

Before surfacing a competitor ask:

1. Would a customer realistically choose this business instead?

2. Does this business meaningfully compete for the same work?

3. Does it strengthen customer trust?

4. Is it relevant to this customer's market?

5. Does it add unique competitive insight?

6. Can we explain why it appears?

7. Is it a stronger competitor than alternatives?

8. Would the business owner immediately recognize it as a legitimate competitor?

If multiple answers are "no,"

the competitor should not be surfaced.

---

# Non-Negotiables

The competitor discovery system will never intentionally:

- prioritize geography over customer choice
- prioritize Google categories over business reality
- surface duplicate brands
- maximize list size at the expense of quality
- recommend competitors unsupported by evidence
- allow AI to bypass deterministic safeguards
- sacrifice customer trust for algorithmic completeness
- optimize API efficiency over recommendation quality

---

# Closing Principle

Competitor discovery is not about building a list.

It is about proving that MarketForge understands the market the customer operates within.

When owners look at their competitor list, they should immediately believe:

**"MarketForge understands exactly who I compete against."**

That confidence becomes the foundation upon which every future recommendation is built.
