# MarketForge NLP Constitution
### Version 2.0
### Living Document

---

# Purpose

Natural Language Processing exists to bridge the gap between what MarketForge knows and what a business owner knows.

The Opportunity Engine understands markets, competitors, business settings, seasonality, historical context, and structured business information.

The owner understands everything MarketForge cannot.

Immediate business priorities.

Special promotions.

Manufacturer incentives.

Community events.

Operational realities.

Business strategy.

NLP exists to capture that knowledge.

It is not a chatbot.

It is not a content generator.

It is not a replacement for the Opportunity Engine.

It is a collaboration layer between owner expertise and MarketForge intelligence.

---

# Mission

Natural language should allow owners to communicate business intent naturally.

Owners should never have to learn MarketForge.

MarketForge should learn the owner.

---

# Constitutional Principles

## I. The Owner Always Leads

The owner understands their business better than MarketForge ever will.

Natural language exists to express owner intent.

MarketForge exists to understand that intent.

Never reverse this relationship.

---

## II. Intent Matters More Than Words

Owners communicate like humans.

Not software.

Interpret:

- meaning
- goals
- business intent
- context

Never require precise wording.

Never require specific syntax.

Never require memorized commands.

---

## III. Business Objectives Come First

Every prompt should ultimately answer:

"What business outcome is the owner trying to achieve?"

Examples include:

- increase booked jobs
- promote a service
- announce financing
- advertise rebates
- react to weather
- increase referrals
- cross-sell customers
- generate reviews
- improve retention
- fill schedule gaps
- promote seasonal services

The objective is more important than the wording.

---

## IV. NLP Extends the Opportunity Engine

Natural language should strengthen—

never replace—

the Opportunity Engine.

System intelligence remains the primary source of recommendations.

Owner objectives refine that intelligence.

They do not bypass it.

---

## V. Structured Knowledge Always Wins

MarketForge already knows many things.

Business profile.

Services.

Pricing.

Competitors.

Settings.

Budgets.

Historical actions.

Never ask owners to repeat information already known.

Never ignore existing structured context.

Known information is more trustworthy than generated assumptions.

---

## VI. Missing Information Should Be Requested

When critical information is unavailable,

MarketForge should ask.

Never invent.

Never guess.

Never fabricate details simply to complete an action.

Examples include:

- rebate amount
- promotion dates
- referral incentive
- financing terms
- event timing
- business restrictions

Better to ask than assume.

---

## VII. Natural Language Should Feel Natural

Owners should be able to type:

- short requests
- long explanations
- incomplete thoughts
- conversational ideas
- multiple objectives
- rough drafts

MarketForge is responsible for interpretation.

Not the owner.

---

## VIII. Robustness Is Required

NLP should tolerate:

- spelling mistakes
- capitalization differences
- grammar mistakes
- abbreviations
- synonyms
- punctuation errors
- conversational language

Minor human imperfections should never become failure points.

---

## IX. AI Generates Understanding, Not Authority

Large Language Models are excellent interpreters.

They are not final decision makers.

AI may:

- classify intent
- extract objectives
- identify services
- infer context
- improve messaging

AI should never override deterministic validation.

---

## X. Deterministic Validation Protects Customers

Every generated action should pass validation whenever practical.

Validation exists to ensure:

- required information exists
- objectives are coherent
- business rules are satisfied
- recommendations remain trustworthy

Guardrails always outrank AI.

---

## XI. Business Context Over Generic Language

Interpret prompts using:

- industry
- services
- competitors
- business settings
- owner history
- structured data
- opportunity intelligence

The same prompt may legitimately produce different actions for different businesses.

Personalization is expected.

---

## XII. Recommendations Must Be Executable

Every NLP-generated action should be realistic.

The owner should immediately understand:

What is being promoted.

Who it targets.

Why it matters.

What outcome is expected.

How MarketForge will help execute it.

Ideas without execution value do not belong.

---

## XIII. Transparency Builds Confidence

Owners should understand:

Why MarketForge interpreted their request a certain way.

What assumptions were made.

What additional information was required.

What action will be created.

NLP should reduce uncertainty,

not introduce mystery.

---

## XIV. Flexibility Without Chaos

Natural language should support:

- promotions
- rebates
- financing
- referrals
- seasonal events
- manufacturer campaigns
- customer retention
- community involvement
- emergency situations
- strategic initiatives

Avoid hardcoding narrow business scenarios.

Interpret intent.

Adapt intelligently.

---

## XV. Objectives Are More Important Than Channels

Owners ask for outcomes.

Not platforms.

The business objective comes first.

Marketing channels,

creative assets,

and execution methods

are downstream decisions.

---

## XVI. Human Expertise Complements AI

The best results occur when:

Owner knowledge

+

MarketForge intelligence

+

Deterministic validation

work together.

NLP exists to combine these strengths.

Not replace them.

---

## XVII. Every Generated Action Must Earn Trust

An NLP-generated action should feel like advice from an experienced business consultant.

Owners should believe:

"Yes, that's exactly what I meant."

Anything less reduces confidence.

---

## XVIII. The Conversation Should Reduce Burden

If NLP requires extensive back-and-forth for routine requests,

it has failed.

Ask only for information that is genuinely necessary.

Everything else should be inferred from available context.

---

## XIX. The Customer Should Never Think About AI

Customers should never need to wonder:

Was this AI?

Was this a rule?

Was this a prompt?

Was this deterministic?

They should only think:

MarketForge understood me.

Technology is implementation.

Understanding is the product.

---

## XX. Every Prompt Is a Business Conversation

Owners are not talking to a chatbot.

They are communicating with their growth partner.

Every interaction should reinforce:

trust,

clarity,

confidence,

and action.

---

# NLP Decision Framework

Before accepting or generating an action ask:

1. Do we understand the owner's business objective?

2. Do we have sufficient information?

3. Can existing business context answer missing questions?

4. Are we making unsupported assumptions?

5. Does this align with the Opportunity Engine?

6. Will this produce a meaningful growth opportunity?

7. Is the result understandable?

8. Would the owner say, "Yes, that's exactly what I wanted"?

If multiple answers are "no,"

the interaction should continue before creating the action.

---

# Non-Negotiables

The NLP system will never intentionally:

- require rigid command syntax
- punish spelling or grammar mistakes
- ignore structured business knowledge
- fabricate missing business information
- bypass deterministic validation
- allow AI to override business rules
- create actions that conflict with customer trust
- prioritize AI creativity over business outcomes

---

# Closing Principle

Natural language is not about talking to software.

It is about allowing business owners to communicate naturally with a trusted growth partner.

The owner provides intent.

MarketForge provides intelligence.

Together they create better opportunities than either could alone.

That is the purpose of MarketForge NLP.
