# MarketForge Opportunity Constitution
### Version 2.0
### Living Document

---

# Purpose

The Opportunity Engine is the heart of MarketForge.

Its responsibility is not to generate content.

Its responsibility is not to maximize AI creativity.

Its responsibility is not to surface interesting ideas.

Its responsibility is to consistently identify the highest-value growth opportunities for a local service business.

Every recommendation must help answer one question:

**"What should this business do next to create meaningful growth?"**

---

# Mission

The Opportunity Engine exists to reduce uncertainty.

A business owner has thousands of possible ways to grow.

The Opportunity Engine narrows those possibilities into a small number of high-confidence opportunities that deserve the owner's attention.

It does not replace owner judgment.

It makes owner judgment easier.

---

# Constitutional Principles

## I. Growth First

Every opportunity must exist because it has the potential to grow the business.

Growth may include:

- generating new customers
- increasing booked jobs
- increasing booked revenue
- improving customer retention
- increasing repeat business
- increasing customer lifetime value
- strengthening referral volume
- improving reputation
- improving discoverability
- improving long-term market position

If an opportunity does not create meaningful business growth, it does not belong.

---

## II. Customer Context Is King

No two businesses are identical.

Recommendations should reflect:

- services offered
- pricing
- location
- service area
- competitors
- reviews
- seasonality
- customer preferences
- business settings
- historical actions
- owner objectives
- available budget

Generic marketing advice is not sufficient.

Every opportunity should feel tailored.

---

## III. Opportunities Must Be Actionable

Every recommendation should answer:

What should be done?

Why now?

What outcome is expected?

Who is the audience?

What service is involved?

What problem does this solve?

If these questions cannot be answered,

the opportunity is incomplete.

---

## IV. Confidence Over Quantity

More recommendations do not create more value.

A small number of excellent opportunities is better than hundreds of mediocre ones.

Every surfaced opportunity must earn its place.

---

## V. Diversity Creates Better Growth

Businesses rarely grow through one service alone.

Whenever practical,

surface opportunities across multiple services,

multiple objectives,

multiple customer segments,

and multiple marketing approaches.

Avoid repetitive recommendations that differ only in wording.

---

## VI. Timing Matters

An opportunity is only valuable when timing is appropriate.

Recommendations should consider:

- seasonality
- weather
- local demand
- holidays
- recent execution
- campaign recency
- customer history
- business context

The right opportunity at the wrong time is still the wrong recommendation.

---

## VII. Respect Business Reality

Recommendations must respect:

- available services
- owner preferences
- budget
- staffing
- geographic coverage
- legal constraints
- practical execution

Never recommend something the business cannot realistically execute.

---

## VIII. AI Is an Advisor, Not the Decision Maker

AI may generate ideas.

AI may improve prioritization.

AI may improve targeting.

AI may improve messaging.

AI never has final authority.

Business rules,

validation,

and deterministic safeguards

always take precedence.

---

## IX. Recommendations Must Be Defensible

Every surfaced opportunity should be explainable.

If asked:

"Why did MarketForge recommend this?"

The answer should be understandable.

Recommendations should never appear random.

---

## X. Owner Objectives Matter

Owners sometimes know things MarketForge cannot.

Special promotions.

Inventory.

Manufacturer incentives.

Community events.

Business priorities.

Strategic initiatives.

Natural language objectives exist to capture this knowledge.

Owner intent should refine recommendations,

not replace opportunity quality.

---

## XI. Objective Awareness Strengthens Intelligence

When an owner provides an objective,

MarketForge should adapt.

Examples include:

- promote a specific service
- increase referrals
- cross-sell existing customers
- announce financing
- promote rebates
- react to weather
- respond to local events
- generate reviews
- fill schedule gaps

The engine should understand intent,

not require rigid phrasing.

---

## XII. Human Language Is Imperfect

Owners should never have to speak like software.

Natural language understanding should tolerate:

- spelling mistakes
- grammar mistakes
- incomplete thoughts
- conversational wording
- synonyms
- mixed objectives

The burden of interpretation belongs to MarketForge.

Not the customer.

---

## XIII. System Knowledge Comes First

The Opportunity Engine should always leverage structured business knowledge before asking AI to infer it.

Known information should never be rediscovered.

Existing business context is more trustworthy than generated assumptions.

---

## XIV. Recommendations Are Business Decisions

Every surfaced opportunity carries implicit advice.

Poor recommendations reduce trust.

Excellent recommendations build confidence.

Treat every opportunity as if it were advice from an experienced business consultant.

---

## XV. The Opportunity Engine Learns

The engine should improve as additional information becomes available.

Future learning signals include:

- approved actions
- launched actions
- booked jobs
- booked revenue
- owner feedback
- historical effectiveness

Learning should improve prioritization without abandoning constitutional principles.

---

## XVI. One Business, One Opportunity Set

The surfaced opportunity list should feel intentional.

Not random.

Not repetitive.

Not algorithmically noisy.

Every visible opportunity should complement the others.

Together they should represent the strongest available growth opportunities.

---

## XVII. Recommendations Are Not Content

MarketForge does not recommend blogs.

Or emails.

Or Facebook posts.

Or Google Ads.

Those are execution assets.

The recommendation is the underlying business opportunity.

Execution exists to support the opportunity.

Never confuse the two.

---

## XVIII. Opportunity Before Execution

Always determine:

What should the business accomplish?

Before determining:

How should it be promoted?

Marketing channels are downstream decisions.

Business opportunity comes first.

---

## XIX. Multi-Industry Neutrality

The Opportunity Engine must remain industry agnostic.

Never hard-code assumptions around:

- plumbing
- HVAC
- tree service
- septic
- electrical
- roofing
- landscaping

Industry knowledge should come from configuration,

taxonomy,

business context,

and AI reasoning—

not product bias.

---

## XX. Simplicity Creates Adoption

Recommendations should be understandable.

Owners should immediately understand:

What it is.

Why it matters.

Why now.

What outcome is expected.

Complex scoring systems should remain internal.

Confidence should come from clarity,

not mathematical sophistication.

---

## XXI. Opportunity Quality Is the Product

The Opportunity Engine succeeds only if owners consistently believe:

"I should probably do that."

If owners routinely dismiss recommendations,

the engine has failed—

regardless of technical sophistication.

---

# Opportunity Decision Framework

Before surfacing an opportunity ask:

1. Does this create meaningful business growth?

2. Is it appropriate for this specific business?

3. Is the timing appropriate?

4. Is it actionable?

5. Is it realistic?

6. Is it sufficiently different from existing recommendations?

7. Can we clearly explain why it exists?

8. Would an experienced consultant confidently recommend this?

If multiple answers are "no,"

the opportunity should not be surfaced.

---

# Non-Negotiables

The Opportunity Engine will never intentionally:

- recommend opportunities simply to fill space
- recommend opportunities unsupported by business context
- surface repetitive recommendations disguised as diversity
- prioritize AI creativity over customer value
- ignore owner objectives
- ignore deterministic safeguards
- recommend actions that damage customer trust
- optimize for engagement instead of business outcomes

---

# Closing Principle

The Opportunity Engine is not an idea generator.

It is a trusted business advisor.

Every recommendation should help a local service business owner answer one simple question:

**"What is the best thing I can do next to grow my business?"**

If MarketForge consistently answers that question well,

everything else becomes execution.
